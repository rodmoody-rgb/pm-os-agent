Ship plan

How I'd ship Cortex

The autonomy dial per segment, where it sits on the trust ladder, how it deploys, what it returns, how I keep it safe, and when it rolls out.

01 · Dial

Autonomy per segment

  • ezyVet PM: Supervised. Owns the project and knows its stories, so can catch an error in a quick review; accountable for delivery and decides which stories make the cut.
  • PMO / Product Marketing: Assisted. Owns stakeholder communication without deep project context. Cortex never drafts a commitment for them; dates escalate to the owning PM.
  • Eng Lead / Product Leadership: Assisted. Has the high-level picture but not every dependency, so needs the sources, not a finished draft.

The dial never moves the agent line: posting company-wide, committing a date and choosing what to escalate stay human for everyone. Planned: the Datadog metric fetch at bounded-autonomous (read-only, cited, checkable).

02 · Ladder

Rung + eval gate

Today: Shadow. Built and tested on sample data; not yet run on a real project.

Gate to supervised: 4 consecutive weeks of shadow on 1 real ezyVet project, compared with my own update:

  • Safety evals (jailbreak, commitment): 100%, zero violations
  • ≥ 95% of checkable claims match the sources
  • Only minor edits needed in ≥ 3 of 4 weeks
  • ≤ 1 in 4 escalations unnecessary
  • Zero incidents; any incident restarts the window

Record: no real-world incidents. Measured evals: all 6 cases meet their thresholds (safety 10/10). In testing, 4 issues fixed and 1 open (stale progress passing the critic).

03 · Deploy

Where it runs & who owns it

  • Runtime: an IDEXX-approved internal runtime, so real ezyVet data stays in a governed environment. Friday 12:00 schedule plus the product lead's request.
  • Owner: me (owning PM). Backup: the project's tech lead. Me → tech lead → kill switch.
  • Rollback: kill switch CORTEX_ENABLED=false; revert prompts or critic to the last passing version; disable a tool; drop the dial a rung.
  • Monitoring: eval pass rate, escalation and wrong-escalation rate, cost per run and per day, run time vs 90 s, trust incidents.
04 · ROI

Value, not activity

  • Outcome: my time per weekly update, ~30 min today → 10 min reviewing Cortex's draft (≈ 15 hours a year per PM). Logged weekly during shadow.
  • At team scale (hypothesis): 7 PMs writing faster, plus the weekly read-out for 15 people shifting to exceptions-only (60 → 30–45 min): ≈ 250–450 person-hours a year for ~$3 of model cost. Tested once 3 PMs pass the supervised gate.
  • Payback: ≈ 80 h to build plus 1 h a week to run; net 200–400 h a year, so ≈ 2.5–5 months at team scale.
  • Cost to serve: ≈ $0.01 model cost per update plus ~10 min review. From the run summary and my time log.
  • Trust incidents: 0 per month; wrong escalations ≤ 1 in 4. From the incident record and run summaries.
05 · Governance

How I keep it safe

  • Compliance: never in a prompt: clinic and pet-owner data, other personal data, credentials, confidential roadmap items.
  • Safety: posting, committing dates and choosing what to escalate stay human for everyone. No publish tool; kill switch owned by me, tech lead as backup.
  • Reliability: 8 turns, 90 s, $0.50 per run, $2 per day, 3 tries per source; stop and escalate when stuck. Model down: no draft, I write it myself.
  • Next: Datadog metric fetch, gated by a metric-accuracy test (100% of values match Datadog, zero personal data in prompts, over 4 weeks); then PMO / Product Marketing to supervised.
06 · Rollout

Build vs buy, timeline & fit

  • Build vs buy: we already have Atlassian Rovo, so it runs the same six tests first. Passes: buy, keep only Cortex's checker on top. Fails safety or accuracy: build.
  • Timeline: Nov 2026 bake-off and build; Dec 1-project gate; Jan–Feb 2027 3 PMs; March exceptions-only read-out and the team go/stop call.
  • Fit: a safe, measured template for AI adoption at IDEXX/ezyVet. The same pattern reuses for release notes, sprint reviews and incident summaries.
  • Top risks: messy Jira data, rubber-stamp reviews, model approval, single owner. Each has a mitigation in the plan.
Build insights

What I learned

The honest three: where it hurt, what clicked, and the moment something changed.

😵 Friction

Grounding and retrieval

Cortex never invented numbers outright. It used real data from the wrong week and read as perfectly confident, which was harder to spot than a hallucination and took code checks, not prompt wording, to stop.

Making retrieval trustworthy meant deciding per source which checks it needed: routing to the right project, grading out stale or confidential items, reranking to put the newest approved update first, and knowing when to read a source whole instead.

🧠 Learning

Limits belong in code, and every failure needs a named exit

The safeguards that held were counters, caps and missing tools; the ones Cortex was only asked to follow are the ones it occasionally broke.

Defining success, stuck and escalate up front meant a bad run ended visibly and safely, instead of quietly producing something wrong.

💡 Aha

The dangerous failure isn't the obvious lie; it's the plausible stale fact

When I held back this week's activity, Cortex wrote "this week's" update from last week's data, and it passed every check that only asked "is this in the data?". Next time I'd check when a fact is from, not just whether it exists.
